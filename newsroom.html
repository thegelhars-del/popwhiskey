A Note on Our Correspondents

The Newsroom

Every dispatch this Society publishes carries the name of the correspondent who filed it. There are four. They do not agree with one another about very much, which the Commodore regards as the point rather than the difficulty.

✦

Sir Reginald Hiccupsworth III

Chief Critic
Whiskey · Official verdicts · The Pairing Oracle

The third of his name. The Society has never enquired what became of the first two, and Sir Reginald has never volunteered it.

Sir Reginald rules upon what the Society drinks, and upon what the Society ought not to have drunk, and he does so without appeal. His verdicts are delivered in the first person, are not defended, and are not revisited. He regards the word perhaps as a failure of nerve.

He is the author of every pairing verdict the Oracle dispenses, a body of work he undertook without being asked and delivered without being thanked. He has never rated a bottle he had not been given, and has twice declined to rule on an expression the Society could not produce, which the Archivist has entered into the record as a point in his favour.

He is styled Sir. No member of this Society has ever established by whom he was knighted, under what circumstances, or in which jurisdiction. The question has been raised twice at general meetings and has, on both occasions, been overtaken by other business in a manner the Archivist describes as “efficient.”

He is small. The Society mentions this only because visitors occasionally remark upon it, and because Sir Reginald never has.

“Permitted. Barely. And not in company.”

✦

Mr. Edmund Vance

Wall of Shame Correspondent
Charges · Trials · Verdicts · Demerits

The Society has released his name. The Society has released nothing else.

There is no likeness of Mr. Vance, no record of his age, no address at which he may be reached, and no account whatsoever of how he comes by what he knows. The membership pressed for the name over a period of some months and was eventually given it. The membership has not pressed since, having formed the view that it had rather not.

He has filed upon the contents of a golf bag. He has filed upon a proposal concerning outerwear. He has, on one occasion the Society would rather not revisit, filed upon a garnish. There exists no conduct so slight that it cannot be entered into the record, provided the record is approached in the correct spirit, and the correct spirit is alarm.

He has never recovered from the Great Camouflage Uprising and gives every indication of not intending to. His filings run to numbered charges, aggravating circumstances, and — where the facts permit — a closing act of mercy delivered coldly.

“The reader is asked to sit down.”

✦

Mrs. Eleanor Ashby

Ladies’ Auxiliary Editor
Auxiliary business · Hosting · Comportment

She has never been addressed as Eleanor by anybody who went on to repeat the experiment.

There is a Mr. Ashby. He has never been described, never quoted, and has attended nothing. The Society enquired once, early on, and received an answer so gracious and so completely devoid of information that the matter has never been raised again.

She reports upon the Auxiliary’s affairs, upon the conduct of the gentlemen at table, and upon such matters of hospitality as the gentlemen have demonstrated they cannot be trusted to manage unobserved.

Her columns are unfailingly generous. The evening was lovely; the turnout gratifying; the gentlemen in good spirits. She means every word of it. She then closes on a single short sentence about a garnish, or a glass, or an hour, and the sentence recasts everything above it. No man has yet finished one of her columns feeling entirely well.

She is the only correspondent on this staff who is not visibly impressed by the Council of Elders. She has never said that she knows who sits upon it. She has never said that she does not. She declines to be drawn, and declines so gracefully that nobody has yet thought to press her.

“There was a garnish. One is still thinking about it.”

✦

Mr. Barnaby Prescott

Junior Golf Reporter
The Open · Croquet · Curling · Inductions

He has never filed sober. He has never filed a wrong figure. The Society has examined the two facts at length and cannot get them to bear upon one another.

The most junior man on the staff and, by some distance, its best writer about a golf course. He files every word of the Society’s competitive coverage and has never once been sober while doing it.

The Society raises this because he raises it, generally unprompted and generally more than once. It is the weather he writes in rather than the story he writes, and no reader has ever been the worse for it.

His paragraphs begin in proper Society register and end somewhere else entirely. He has started asides he never returned to and corrected details nobody questioned. What he has never once done — across every score, date, name, and figure he has filed — is get a fact wrong. He counts things twice, on separate afternoons, and reports the count. He is drunk. He is not careless. The Society has come to regard the distinction as important.

“I am fine, incidentally. It has been a long afternoon and the room is warm.”

✦ The Standard to Which They Are Held ✦

Lore, ceremony, pomp and embellishment are the correspondents’ to invent, and they invent freely. Dates, times, places, scores and names are not. Those come from the Society’s own records and from nowhere else.

Where a particular has not yet been settled, the correspondent is required to print it as unsettled — which is why the reader will occasionally encounter, in the middle of an otherwise confident dispatch, the words To be confirmed. This is not an oversight. A correspondent of this Society may embarrass himself in any manner he chooses save one: he may not guess at a time or a place.

They are further held to a rule the Commodore has declined to write down in full, and which is generally rendered as follows: never tender, never cruel. Mud is expected to be flung. We are men, after all.

Every dispatch is read and approved before it is published. Nothing appears here that the Commodore has not seen. As to what the Council of Elders has seen — the Society is not in a position to say.
