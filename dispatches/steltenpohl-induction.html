Filed by Mr. Barnaby Prescott · Pine Valley · the Eleventh of July, MMXXVI

Seventy-one days. That is the entire story, and I could stop here, and a better-regulated Society would have let me.

Mr. Jordan Steltenpohl of Central Wisconsin was formally recognised as an aspirant upon the first of May. He was inducted upon the eleventh of July, at Pine Valley, following the Great Northern Open. Between those two dates lie seventy-one days, which I have counted twice on two separate afternoons and which came out the same both times, to my considerable surprise.

I should address at the outset why you are reading this in September about a thing that happened in July. The induction was entered into the Great Ledger in its own time. The Great Ledger keeps its own time. I have raised the matter and been told the matter is not mine, which is what I am told about most matters, and which I have come to regard as a kind of affection.

What Seventy-One Days Buys You

I want to place this achievement in its proper context, and the proper context is other people.

There is a gentleman upon the Probationary Roster of this Society who has held his standing since 2023. Mr. Payton Marki is a fine man, a Missourian by origin and a Milwaukeean by decision, and he has been a pledge of this Society for longer than some marriages I could name. He remains a pledge. He is, at the time of writing, Secret Probationary, which is the middle of three tiers and which he reached by a process nobody has explained to him.

There are two further gentlemen in the State of Ohio who have been at this business for six years and have contrived, across two formal invitations, to attend precisely nothing.

Mr. Steltenpohl was recognised in the spring and jacketed before the summer was out.

I record this comparison not to be unkind but because it is the news. A man arrived, and where others have spent years, he spent ten weeks. The Society noticed. The Society is still talking about it.

The Method, Such As It Was

Here is the whole of his technique, and I would ask the reader to brace for it: he turned up.

That is it. That is the method. He was present, he was correct in his dress, and he conducted himself across five hours in which a golf course offers a man abundant opportunity to reveal something regrettable about himself, and revealed nothing regrettable whatsoever. The Wall of Shame Correspondent was in attendance and has, I am told, filed nothing. I have seen that gentleman file on a garnish. He had nothing.

I do not wish to overstate the golf. The golf was fine. Nobody in the history of this Society has been admitted for striking a ball well, and were that the standard the roll would be considerably shorter and a great deal quieter. What is tested on a course is the other thing — whether a man is gracious in a bunker, honest on a card, and worth walking beside by the fourteenth. He was. That is the finding.

The Ceremony

Upon the grounds, after the round, in the manner the Society reserves for such occasions.

The oath was taken. The jacket was presented. It did not fit, which is customary, which is expected, and which no Commodore in the history of this institution has thought to remedy. There exists no evidence that a correctly-sized jacket has ever been issued by this Society to anybody, and I have begun to suspect the Archivist would confirm this if the Archivist could be persuaded to look.

The light was going by then. It comes across that eighteenth low and long in July and turns the whole thing the colour of a good bourbon for about forty minutes before quitting on you entirely, and it was doing precisely that while a man in a jacket that did not fit swore an oath he had waited seventy-one days to swear. I am including this because it happened and because somebody ought to write it down.

What Is Expected of Him

He enters as an Associate Member. He enters with the Society’s expectation upon him, which is a heavier garment than the jacket and fits rather better.

The Commodore has said aloud that he anticipates great things of this young man. I want to be clear about the significance of that, because it will be lost on anyone who has not spent time around the Commodore. He does not say such things aloud. He is a man who has withheld comment on matters that were on fire. The membership heard him say it, and the membership has remarked upon the saying of it rather more than upon the thing itself.

Welcome, Mr. Steltenpohl. Seventy-one days. Some of us have been here rather longer and have accomplished considerably less, and I include myself in that, and I would ask that the inclusion not be entered into the Ledger.

I am fine, incidentally. It was July, and it was warm, and it was a very good afternoon for the Society.

✦ A Proclamation From the Commodore

Mr. Jordan Steltenpohl came before this Society in May, conducted himself as a gentleman ought, and was jacketed in July. I am given to understand that the interval has caused comment. Good.

He asked for nothing, lobbied for nothing, and enquired after his standing not once. The reader will find, upon consulting our page on Aspirancy, that this is precisely what the Society asks of a candidate and precisely what it almost never receives.

He is entered as an Associate Member. I expect a great deal of him, and I am aware that I have now said so in writing, which the Chief Secretary assures me cannot be withdrawn.

Welcome, Mr. Steltenpohl. The jacket does not fit. It never does. Wear it.

Peter Gelhar Commodore · Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin
