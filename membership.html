Scott Lange

Member · Father of Matthew Lange · Official Insurance Expert · Vodka Man in a Whiskey World · Future Father-in-Law of the Founder's Daughter

There is a vetting process in the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin. It is not always formal. It is not always written down. But it exists, it is real, and it is administered by a Council of Elders whose collective judgment has, over the years, proven remarkably sound. When Scott Lange's name came before that council, there was a moment of deliberation. A pause. A raised eyebrow or two. Because the intelligence had come in, and the intelligence was clear.

The man preferred vodka.

The council considered this. The bylaws were consulted. The membership murmured. And then, in a moment of collective wisdom that speaks to the Society's fundamental generosity of spirit, someone said the quiet part out loud — we have a Deacon who drinks beer, a father-in-law who orders Old Fashioneds, a health coach who probably shouldn't be here at all, and an uncle who nearly replaced the tweed with camouflage. Does the vodka really disqualify him?

It did not. Scott Lange was cleared for induction. The Society has never looked back.

The sponsor in this case was none other than Matt Lange — inaugural curling champion, future son-in-law of the founder, and a young man who has always demonstrated an uncanny instinct for knowing exactly which card to play and when. Sponsoring his own father was perhaps his most elegant move yet.

Scott was not, by his own implicit admission, immediately convinced that the Society was entirely legitimate. There was a period during which Scott surveyed the assembled brotherhood, took in the ceremonies, the proceedings, the jackets, the Oracle of the Cask, the wheel, the bylaws, and arrived at the quiet but firm conclusion that something here might be slightly off about these people. This is an understandable reaction. Many of the Society's finest members have passed through this exact phase.

What changed Scott's mind was a summer afternoon on a golf course — the warm Wisconsin sun, the green fairways, the simple pleasure of a good round among genuinely good men. Something settled in Scott Lange that day. The skepticism softened. The jacket began to feel less like a costume and more like it belonged.

Scott is one of the Society's quieter members — a man who does not fill a room with volume but fills it with something more valuable. Steadiness. Presence. The particular quality of a man who listens more than he speaks and therefore, when he does speak, is worth listening to.

Scott Lange is, by formal Society designation, the Official Insurance Expert of the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin. Should the Society ever need to file a claim — for a curling incident, perhaps, or damages arising from certain sartorial uprisings best left unnamed — Scott Lange will be the first call made. The Society sleeps better knowing this.

He came in uncertain. He stayed because of the golf course. He remained because of the people. The vodka, it turns out, was never the point. Welcome to the family, Scott. Both of them.

Noah Miesfeld

Member · Class of 2024 · Pledge of Matt Lange · Of Sheboygan Lineage · Chicago Insurance Man

Mr. Noah Miesfeld came to the Society in the manner of all sound things: slowly, by way of patient observation, and on the recommendation of a man whose word the Commodore has long since ceased to question. That man was Mr. Matt Lange, himself once a pledge of this body and now among its most reliable architects of consequence. Mr. Lange vouched. The Commodore considered. The matter, in due course, was settled.

It would be an oversight bordering on negligence to introduce Mr. Miesfeld without acknowledging the territory from which he hails. Sheboygan, Wisconsin — a city whose contributions to the gentleman's table are, in the matter of cured and smoked meats, of European stature. The Miesfeld name occupies a position within that tradition which the Society shall describe as consequential, and which other and lesser publications have, with characteristic imprecision, described as royal. The Society does not traffic in the language of monarchies. The Society does, however, recognise an heir when one presents himself, and it acknowledges, without further elaboration, that Mr. Miesfeld arrives at our gatherings with a pedigree that announces itself before he does.

Mr. Miesfeld's professional life is conducted, by his own arrangement, in the city of Chicago, where he is engaged in matters pertaining to insurance — a discipline the Society respects for its quiet usefulness and its tolerance of long lunches. The membership notes, with the candour that befits a body of this character, that several of its number maintain with Chicago a relationship best described as fraught, particularly where the city's sporting institutions are concerned. The Society has examined this circumstance from multiple angles and concluded that Mr. Miesfeld's residence in that city, far from disqualifying him, in fact equips him uniquely to receive the membership's commentary on the subject without taking it personally. This, too, is a form of fitness for membership.

On matters of the game: Mr. Miesfeld is a golfer of demonstrated capability, of the sort that earns its standing not through proclamation but through the slow accumulation of well-struck shots witnessed by men who would have noticed otherwise. More notably — and the Society finds this detail genuinely commendatory — Mr. Miesfeld has spent time in service as a caddie, a station the Society considers among the most clarifying any young man may occupy. To caddie is to observe. To observe is to understand. To understand is, in the fullness of time, to be in possession of knowledge which the men paying for one's services rarely suspect one has acquired. Mr. Miesfeld bestowed this knowledge generously upon those who sought it, and, the Society suspects, more sparingly upon those who did not but ought to have. Either disposition speaks well of him.

Mr. Miesfeld was inducted in 2024 following a period of pledgeship the Society shall not detail here, save to note that it concluded as such periods properly conclude: with the candidate's name added to the rolls and the matter never again raised in his presence. He is, in Mr. Lange, possessed of a friend of the most loyal sort, and the Society has come to expect that where one of these gentlemen is to be found, the other shall not be far. The membership has no objection to this arrangement and, on the matter of pours, certain operational conveniences.

The Society welcomes Mr. Miesfeld and considers him, on present evidence, an asset to its proceedings. The pedigree is acknowledged. The city is forgiven. The pours, in his presence, have not suffered.

Jason Bade

Member · Class of 2024 · Sponsored by Randy Fisher · Natural Born Member · Victim of the Tractor Beam

There is a gravitational force that surrounds the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin. It is invisible, it is patient, and it is absolutely inevitable. Linger long enough in the Society's orbit — attend enough gatherings, know enough members, spend enough time in the warm glow of the brotherhood's considerable hospitality — and the tractor beam engages. Resistance is not merely futile. It is physically impossible.

Jason Bade never really stood a chance. And the Society means that as the highest possible compliment.

The circumstances of Jason's arrival follow a pattern the Society has come to recognize — the trusted sponsor, the established relationship, the quiet but unmistakable nod of approval from someone whose judgment the membership respects completely. In this case, that someone was Randy Fisher — Keeper of the Homeland, Patron Saint of the Relocation Bar, one of the Society's most foundational original members. When Randy Fisher sponsors a man, the Society listens.

But Randy's sponsorship tells only part of the story. Jason Bade arrived as the man in the life of Randy's sister — co-owner of the beloved Relocation Bar, cornerstone of one of the Society's most cherished families. To love someone from that family is to already understand something fundamental about what this Society values. Jason understood it instinctively. This was noted. This mattered.

And a natural fit is precisely what Jason Bade is. He arrived, he settled in, and he belonged. Just like that. The man moves through a room the way a well-aged dram moves across the palate — smoothly, warmly, and leaving everyone around him feeling better than they did before.

On the golf course Jason is exactly the kind of partner the Society prizes — present, engaged, genuinely enjoying the round for what it is. He hangs with the boys with the easy confidence of a man who has always known how to be good company without trying too hard. This is rarer than it sounds.

And then there is the matter of his character — which, by every account from every member who has spent time with Jason Bade, is simply and genuinely excellent. Super nice, they say, with the slightly amazed tone of people who keep expecting to find the catch and keep coming up empty. There is no catch. Jason is exactly what he appears to be.

He lingered in the orbit. The tractor beam engaged. The jacket was presented at the 2024 Great Northern Open and Jason Bade took his rightful place among the brotherhood. The Society did not have to think twice. It rarely does when Randy Fisher is vouching.

Jeff Mohring

Member · Class of 2024 · GNO Inductee · Father of the Family · Reluctant Champion · Old Fashioned Connoisseur

There are men who pursue membership in the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin with open enthusiasm. There are men who linger patiently at the periphery until the moment is right. And then there is Jeff Mohring — a man who, by all available evidence, was actively attempting to avoid the whole thing right up until the moment a golf cart pulled up, the Wisconsin sun beat down on the fairways of Greenwood Hills, and the Society finally, definitively, lovingly closed the trap.

All it took was a nice day and a golf course. Jeff never saw it coming. Or perhaps he did, and simply ran out of runway. Either way, the jacket was presented at the 2024 Great Northern Open and Jeff Mohring became an official member of the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin, begrudgingly or otherwise. The Society prefers to think otherwise.

The story of Jeff Mohring and this Society is not, however, a story that begins in 2024. It begins much earlier — in 2016, when the threads of two families began quietly weaving themselves together. His daughter Natalie and the founder's daughter Maisie crossed paths on a high school sports field, and what started as the ordinary overlap of two Wisconsin families gradually revealed itself to be something far more permanent. Natalie went on to marry the founder's son Cade — himself a proud Society member — making Jeff Mohring not merely a member but a cornerstone of the extended Gelhar family constellation.

Jeff brings to the Society the particular quality of a man who has been around long enough to know exactly what he thinks, exactly what he likes, and exactly how he wants to spend a good afternoon. On the golf course, he is a willing and dependable partner — the kind of man whose company makes the round better regardless of the scorecard.

Now. The whiskey. Jeff's relationship with the Society's sacred elixir is best described as respectful but not exclusive. His heart belongs to the Old Fashioned — that most Wisconsin of cocktails, that perfect marriage of bourbon, bitters, sugar, and the particular Midwestern sensibility that says a good drink should be exactly what it is without apology or unnecessary complexity. In Wisconsin, the Old Fashioned is not merely a cocktail. It is a cultural institution. A birthright. And Jeff Mohring honors that tradition with the conviction of a true believer.

Beneath the reluctance — beneath the careful distance of a man who spent several years successfully avoiding formal induction — there is pride. It is not loud pride. It is the quiet, deep, entirely genuine pride of a man who finds himself among good people doing something genuinely worth doing, and knows it, even if he would never quite say so out loud.

Jeff Mohring did not chase this brotherhood. This brotherhood caught him on a hot summer afternoon on a golf course in Wausau, Wisconsin, and handed him a jacket he didn't know he needed. He needed it. He knows it now. The Old Fashioned tastes better with brothers around the table.

Scott Markrof

Member · Probably · Status: Unconfirmed · Jacket: Unknown · Whereabouts: Unknown

There are members whose presence defines the Society. There are members whose absence has been formally prosecuted — we see you, Jeff Schuster. And then there is Scott Markrof, who occupies a category so entirely his own that the Society has had to create new philosophical frameworks just to accommodate him.

Is Scott Markrof a member? By all available evidence, yes. By any measurable metric of attendance, confirmation, or jacket possession — the record grows complicated. Scott exists in a quantum state of membership that the Society's bylaws were simply not written to address. He is simultaneously fully a member and completely unaccounted for, and the truly unsettling part is that everyone strongly suspects this is exactly how Scott wants it.

The jacket situation alone keeps the Chief Secretary up at night. There are two possibilities. Either Scott received his jacket at some point during a gathering that the membership collectively cannot reconstruct with any confidence — a testament to either the quality of the whiskey that evening or Scott's extraordinary talent for operating below the radar — or he did not receive one and has simply never needed it acknowledged. Both scenarios are equally plausible. Both are somehow completely on brand.

What the Society knows for certain about Scott Markrof is this: he is, by every account of every member who has encountered him, a genuinely great man. Warm, capable, the kind of person whose presence — however fleeting and however difficult to document — makes the gathering better simply by his having been there. Or near there. Or somewhere in the general vicinity. The details are hazy. The impression endures.

He is a man of the outdoors in the truest sense — comfortable in the wild, at ease in the elements, possessed of the particular quiet confidence that comes from a man who has spent enough time in nature to understand that most of what the rest of us worry about simply does not matter.

The Society has set a formal objective for the coming year: to obtain a confirmed, documented, timestamped photograph of Scott Markrof at an official Society function. A man. A jacket, circumstances permitting. A Glencairn glass or at minimum a beverage of some kind. Proof of existence within the brotherhood's orbit. The membership has been briefed. Everyone is on alert.

Scott, presumably, is already three steps ahead of all of them.

Jeff Schuster was suspended for going AWOL. Scott Markrof turned AWOL into an art form and somehow remains beloved for it. The Society salutes him. Wherever he is.

Maisie Gelhar

Member · Daughter of the Founder · Financial Guru · Keeper of the Books · Curling Captain · One of Two

Let the record show that the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin is, by tradition, custom, and the general disposition of its membership, a gentlemen's organization. Let the record also show that Maisie Gelhar did not ask for an exception. She simply showed up, did something nobody else could do, won, and left the Society with absolutely no choice in the matter.

This is, perhaps, the most Gelhar thing that has ever happened.

There are two women in the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin. Two. In the entire history of this brotherhood — the ceremonies, the proceedings, the curling disasters, the disciplinary hearings, the camouflage uprising, the GNO championships — exactly two women have crossed the threshold and earned the jacket. Maisie Gelhar is one of them. The Society does not hand these out. Maisie did not receive one. She earned it, on the ice, under pressure, in a manner so decisive and so thoroughly documented that the membership had no procedural grounds to argue otherwise and, frankly, wouldn't have dared.

Here is what happened. The Society needed to curl. The Society, it became apparent, did not entirely know how to curl. Enter Maisie Gelhar — who not only knew the game but understood it at a level that left the assembled brotherhood quietly humbled. She did not merely advise. She stepped in, took the captain's position with the calm authority of someone who was always going to be in charge eventually, taught the club what it needed to know, and then led the team to victory.

The founder reviewed the situation. The bylaws were consulted. The precedent was established. The jacket was presented. She better appreciate it.

Beyond the ice, Maisie serves the Society in a capacity that may be its most essential function after the whiskey itself — she is the financial guru and bookkeeper, the woman who ensures that the Society's books are balanced, its accounts are transparent, and its treasury remains above board and beyond reproach. In a club that has, on occasion, made decisions of questionable fiscal prudence in the warm glow of an excellent pour, having Maisie Gelhar watching the numbers is not merely convenient. It is the difference between a thriving Society and a very expensive hobby with no financial records.

And then there is the matter of Matt Lange — fellow member, inaugural curling champion, future son-in-law of the founder, and a man whose own biography notes with some delicacy his extraordinary talent for keeping the founder happy. Maisie is marrying him. Whether this represents Matt's greatest strategic achievement or simply the best thing that ever happened to him is a question the Society leaves to the philosophers.

She is her father's daughter in every way that matters. The Society is lucky to have her. The founder already knew that.

Chad Bowe

Member · Stevens Point Chapter · Resident Beer Snob · Culinary Correspondent · Annapolis Veteran · Worth the Wait

There are members who walked straight from introduction to induction without so much as a speed bump. And then there is Chad Bowe — a man who made the pilgrimage to Annapolis, stood in the presence of the brotherhood, breathed the same air as the OG crew, absorbed the ceremony and the camaraderie and the considerable gravity of the occasion, and was sent home without a jacket.

Not yet, Chad. Not yet.

To his eternal credit, Chad Bowe did not sulk. He did not petition. He did not launch a formal appeal or arrive at the next gathering with a lawyer — though the Society does have one of those on retainer now, thanks to Neil Doherty. Chad simply went back to Stevens Point, continued being exactly who he is, and waited. Because Chad Bowe is a man who understands that good things — like good beer, like good food, like good friendships — cannot be rushed.

The time became right in 2023. The jacket was presented. The wait, it turned out, had only made it better.

The Chad Bowe origin story begins not in ceremony but in the decidedly more ordinary setting of a shared workplace — two men, a common employer, and the gradual dawning realization on the founder's part that this particular coworker was, in fact, pretty cool. This is how the best friendships begin. Not with grand gestures but with the quiet accumulation of evidence that someone is worth knowing. Chad passed that test comprehensively.

Now. The whiskey. Chad is, by his own proud and unapologetic admission, a beer man. Not merely a beer drinker — a beer snob. A connoisseur of the craft, a student of the pour, a man who approaches a tap list with the same reverence that the Society's finest members bring to a flight of single malts. The whiskey, for now, remains an acquired taste in progress. The Society is patient. There is precedent. There is hope.

What Chad brings to the table in the meantime — literally and figuratively — more than compensates. The man is a culinary bloodhound of the first order. A connoisseur of fine food and a dedicated scout of great finds, Chad moves through the world with the perpetual, delightful alertness of someone always on the lookout for the next remarkable meal, the next hidden gem, the next place that nobody knows about yet but absolutely should. When Chad recommends somewhere, you go.

He is always down for whatever the Society conjures. A man who makes time for the Society despite having every reason not to is a man who actually values it. The Society notices these things.

He made it to Annapolis before he made it into the jacket. That trip, that patience, that willingness to show up even before the Society was ready to officially claim him — that is the measure of Chad Bowe. He was always worth waiting for. The Society just needed a moment to catch up.

Jeff Deviney

Member · THE Captain · Texas Chapter · OG Annapolis Crew · Keeper of Order · Father of the Brotherhood

There are societies that run smoothly by accident. Ours runs smoothly because of Jeff Deviney. And make no mistake — there is nothing accidental about Jeff Deviney.

Inducted as a proud member of the OG Annapolis crew — that legendary 2018 gathering that preceded the Society's very founding, making Jeff one of the rare few who was present before the ink on the bylaws was even dry — Jeff arrived and immediately did what Jeff Deviney does everywhere he goes. He assessed the situation, identified what needed doing, and quietly ensured that everything ran exactly as it should. This is not a personality trait. This is a calling.

Known throughout the brotherhood as THE Captain — not a captain, not one of several captains, but THE Captain, definite article fully intended and non-negotiable — Jeff serves as the Society's disciplinarian and keeper of order with the precision of a man engineered for exactly this purpose. Where others see a gathering of enthusiastic gentlemen enjoying fine whiskey and spirited conversation, Jeff sees a system. A beautiful, manageable, optimizable system of people who need to be in the right place at the right time with the right glass in hand. He makes this happen.

Think of him as the Society's air traffic controller — except instead of planes, he's managing a collection of whiskey-loving gentlemen of varying punctuality and questionable decision-making, and instead of a radar screen he has sheer force of personality and an organizational mind that borders on the supernatural. Somehow, miraculously, everyone ends up where they need to be. That is THE Captain's gift to this brotherhood and it cannot be overstated.

He is always up for the shenanigans. This is important. A man of order and discipline who cannot let his hair down is merely a bureaucrat. Jeff Deviney is something far more interesting — a man who can keep the trains running on time and then be the last one standing at the end of the evening, fully present for every magnificent moment in between. The discipline and the revelry coexist in perfect balance.

Now. Texas. Jeff represents the Lone Star State chapter with a pride so complete, so thoroughly genuine, and so consistently expressed that the membership has simply made peace with the fact that Texas will come up. It always comes up. It should come up. Texas Tech. Texas A&M. The football allegiances run deep and run loud, and Jeff will ensure that you are fully informed on the matter regardless of your level of prior interest. This is not evangelism. This is identity.

He is the father of a fellow Society member — a distinction that places him among the growing ranks of the brotherhood's generational members, men whose legacy in this club runs deeper than their own jacket.

Jeff Deviney was in Annapolis in 2018 before the Society even had a name. He fell in with the right crowd — or the wrong crowd, depending on your perspective — and became an indispensable part of everything that followed. THE Captain. The order keeper. The Texas ambassador. The disciplinarian who can outparty the room. The Society runs tighter because Jeff is in it. Everything does.

Brad Ellingson

Member · Twin Cities Chapter · Health & Fitness Ambassador · Shepherd of the Founder's Minnesota Education

There are friendships that develop slowly over years of careful cultivation. And then there are the friendships that announce themselves immediately — two men on a football practice sideline, watching their sons run drills, realizing within the span of a single conversation that this is someone worth knowing. Brad Ellingson and the founder had that moment. The Society gained a Twin Cities chapter because of it. The founder gained an education in Minnesota living that continues to this day.

Brad represents the Society's Minneapolis-St. Paul contingent with the easy confidence of a man who knows exactly who he is and sees absolutely no contradiction in any of it. Which brings us to the central and delightful paradox of Brad Ellingson — the man is a certified health coach. A dedicated, knowledgeable, genuinely committed ambassador for physical wellness, fitness, and the kind of disciplined lifestyle that keeps a man in exceptional condition. He counts macros. He understands recovery. He knows what your body needs and how to get it there.

He is also a proud and enthusiastic member of a gentlemen's whiskey society. The Society finds this not contradictory but aspirational. If Brad can do both, perhaps there is hope for everyone. He is living proof that a man can appreciate the finer spirits without surrendering the finer version of himself.

On the golf course, Brad's dedication borders on the heroic. He is in Minnesota. The Great Northern Open is in Wausau. This gap — geographic, logistical, entirely surmountable if you care enough — has never once stopped Brad Ellingson from showing up. He makes the journey. He plays the round. He represents the Twin Cities chapter with the commitment of a man who understands that distance is just a number and brotherhood is not optional.

But golf may not be Brad's greatest contribution to the founder's life. That distinction belongs to an entire world Brad personally opened the door to — snowmobile life in Minnesota, Sno Cross racing, and the kind of winter adventures that only someone deeply rooted in the North would think to share. Before Brad, the founder had not fully appreciated what it meant to point a machine at a frozen horizon and simply go. After Brad, he understood completely. This is the gift of a truly good friend — not just companionship but expansion.

And after the snowmobiles, when the cold has done its work and the appetite has been properly earned — Brad introduced the founder to the Lion's Tap in Eden Prairie. A burger of such legendary, unpretentious, magnificent simplicity that it deserves mention in the same breath as the Society's finest drams. Brad knows where the good things are. He always has.

One of the genuinely nicest men this Society has ever inducted — Brad Ellingson arrived as a football practice acquaintance and became something far more permanent. Their sons play together. Their families are connected. The Society stretches from Wausau to the Twin Cities because two fathers stood on a sideline one day and recognized something in each other.

The health coach and the whiskey society. The fitness ambassador and the Lion's Tap burger. The Minnesota winter and the open throttle of a snowmobile at full speed. Brad Ellingson contains multitudes. The Society is richer for every single one of them.

Terry S. Noone

Member · Father of the Founder's Better Half · Grandfather of the Brotherhood · Survivalist in Residence · Keeper of the Mustache That Was

There are members who sought the spotlight. There are members who wandered into it. And then there is Terry Noone — a man who saw the spotlight coming from a considerable distance, assessed it carefully, calculated the optimal safe perimeter, and positioned himself just far enough away to maintain plausible deniability while still being very much present. This is not reluctance. This is strategy. Terry Noone is, above all else, a strategic man.

The circumstances of Terry's induction were, to put it plainly, inevitable. When your daughter marries the founder of a whiskey society — a man of boundless enthusiasm, elaborate ceremony, and an apparently inexhaustible appetite for pomp — there is really only one direction this is going. Terry saw it coming. Terry accepted it with the quiet grace of a man who understands that some things in life are simply beyond negotiation. The jacket was presented. Terry nodded. The Society gained one of its most quietly essential members.

Now. Terry is not a whiskey man by nature or by calling. He will partake on occasion — graciously, without complaint, with the measured cooperation of a man who has learned that sometimes you simply raise the glass — but his heart belongs to the water. Specifically, to whatever body of water currently holds the largest, most elusive, most magnificently stubborn fish in the state of Wisconsin. Terry fishes with abandon. There is no other phrase for it. The man dedicates to the pursuit of freshwater quarry the kind of resources, energy, and logistical commitment that lesser men reserve for mortgages and retirement planning.

And lest anyone mistake Terry's fishing passion for mere recreation — the man speared the largest freshwater sturgeon in the recorded history of the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin. This is not a small distinction. This is a title. This is a legacy. Somewhere in the Society's lore, alongside the GNO champions and the curling victors, sits the name Terry Noone — Slayer of the Great Sturgeon. It will not be forgotten.

Beyond the water, Terry is the Society's resident survivalist — the man you want beside you when civilization becomes optional. He hunts. He fishes. He reads the land the way the founder reads a whiskey menu — with knowledge, with confidence, and with a clear sense of exactly what he's looking for. Golf, however, is another matter entirely. Terry disdains the game with a conviction that the Society respects if not fully shares.

He keeps his distance deliberately. Terry Noone did not come to the Society to be swept up in the whirlwind — he came to observe it, support it when needed, and quietly ensure that the founder's more ambitious ideas don't entirely spiral beyond the bounds of reason. This is an invaluable service. He will do anything for the club. Anything. This is a man who goes along with the founder's schemes with the patient, loving cooperation of someone who has been going along with things for years and has made a quiet peace with the fact that this is simply how life near Peter Gelhar works.

Now. The mustache. There exists — somewhere in the amber glow of living memory — a version of Terry Noone that the brotherhood has taken to calling Mustache Terry. A man of such distinguished facial topiary that the membership has not forgotten it, cannot forget it, and continues at regular intervals to formally request its return. The calls have been made. Motions have been implied. The brethren have spoken with one voice on this matter. Terry has, thus far, rebuffed every overture with the same quiet resolve he applies to everything else in life. The mustache remains retired. The campaign continues.

He is grandfather to a member, father to the woman who keeps the founder grounded, and one of the most genuinely good sports this Society has ever had the privilege of inducting. When the club needs him, Terry Noone shows up. Every time. Without fanfare, without complaint, and without a golf club anywhere in sight.

Mike Gelhar

Member · Oshkosh Chapter · The Second Stooge · Brother of The Deacon · The Reluctant · Keeper of the Punchline

Every great trio has its dynamic. The wise one. The wild one. And the one standing slightly to the side, shaking his head slowly, wondering how he got here and quietly glad that he did. In the legendary Three Stooges of the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin, that man is Mike Gelhar — the Second Stooge, the middle brother, and quite possibly the most reluctant inductee in the entire history of the brotherhood.

And that is saying something. This Society has accumulated reluctant members the way a fine whiskey accumulates complexity — slowly, naturally, and with results that ultimately justify the wait. But Mike occupies a special tier of reluctance. A rarefied altitude. A level of initial resistance so pure and so committed that it practically deserves its own bylaw.

The story goes that in Annapolis — that storied city which has now claimed more Society inductions than any location outside of Wisconsin — Mike Gelhar found himself surrounded by his brothers George and Gary, by the assembled brotherhood, by the particular combination of warmth and peer pressure that the Society has quietly perfected over the years, and by what can only be described as an inevitability. His brothers were members. His nephews had built the thing. The jackets were right there. Resistance, it turned out, was not merely futile — it was geographically impossible.

Mike was convinced. Or persuaded. Or, depending on who is telling the story and how much has been poured, simply outmaneuvered by his own family in a city far from home with no reasonable exit strategy. The Society prefers the word inducted. Mike might choose differently. Either way, the jacket was presented, and the Gelhar dynasty was complete.

Now. Mike is a beer man. Unambiguously, unapologetically, thoroughly a beer man. In a Society built around the appreciation of fine whiskey, Mike has arrived at every gathering with the quiet confidence of a man who knows exactly what he likes and has never once required anyone else's approval of it. There is actually something deeply admirable about this. The bylaws permit it. The membership respects it. And frankly, a man who raises a cold beer in a room full of Glencairn glasses and does so without flinching is a man of genuine character.

But here is what Mike Gelhar brings to the Society that no whiskey and no bylaw could ever replicate. The jokes. The man is, without qualification or close competition, the fastest joke mind in the entire brotherhood. Where others pause, Mike has already arrived. Where others are still processing the setup, Mike has delivered the punchline, waited the perfect beat, and moved on. Mr. Jeffrey Ziegel arrived within the Society's orbit by the gentlest and most reliable of routes: he was, simply, around. It is the sort of family one ends up around accidentally and remains around quite deliberately.

Mr. Derrick Rieger comes to the Society's official rolls in the manner of a man who has, in every meaningful sense, been there all along — by the slow and somewhat embarrassed realisation, on the part of the membership, that he has been with the brotherhood for longer than the brotherhood has had a brotherhood. One does not, the membership has decided, audition the foundation after the house is built.

Mr. Rieger and the Commodore met in Germany in the year of our Lord 1998, ostensibly enrolled in a basic-level German language course. Mr. Rieger's American companions, observing a large and amiable fellow seated among the conjugation exercises, arrived at the only reasonable conclusion the evidence supported: that here was a big, dopey German who had, for reasons unknowable, registered to learn his own mother tongue. The arithmetic did not compute. It was only upon conversation that the truth emerged — the man was from Cincinnati, which the Society, with its characteristically generous cartography, accepted as basically right next door to the Commodore's sister. The friendship was sealed shortly thereafter in a German tavern, over a quantity of beer that history has tactfully declined to record.

In a gesture establishing his bona fides beyond all subsequent dispute, Mr. Rieger drove the following year from Ohio to Oshkosh, Wisconsin to attend the Commodore's wedding, where he ingratiated himself not merely to the groom but to the entire Family — an institution within which he has remained permanently and unreservedly lodged ever since. The Society shall note the distinction, because it is one the membership has had occasion to observe: a friend of the Commodore is a guest, and friends of the Commodore the Society receives in considerable number. A friend of the Commodore's mother is something else entirely. Mr. Rieger has been the latter since the Clinton administration.

On the matter of jurisdictional standing, which one or two of the more procedurally-minded members have raised, the Society shall offer a single reply. Mr. Rieger is a charter member of the Fighting 54th Snowmobile Club, a fraternal order of impeccable seniority, and under the long-standing reciprocity accords between our two institutions, his membership there confers full diplomatic standing here. The matter is closed and shall not be reopened within his hearing.

Mr. Rieger's contributions to the brotherhood have been rendered, in the main, in the wilderness. He has snowmobiled the North Woods. He has snowboarded in conditions of questionable judgment. In the mountains of Colorado he received the Commodore as a guest, where the two engaged in the discharge of fully automatic firearms and the spirited and entirely unsupervised redistribution of certain timber — activities the Society neither confirms, denies, nor in any way memorialises in writing, but which it nonetheless regards as exemplary of the man's character.

It is the considered view of this office that the Society would not be what it is without Mr. Rieger's long and uncatalogued influence upon the Commodore's life, and that the question of his elevation has never been one of whether, but of how grand. He shall receive his jacket at the rehearsal dinner, or whenever next the brotherhood convenes, and he shall receive it as what he has always been: not a newcomer at all, but a cornerstone who was, by no fault of the brotherhood's organisational rigour, simply slow to be informed.

The Society welcomes Mr. Rieger formally and at long last, acknowledges what the brotherhood owes him in matters foundational, and shall keep, against the next convening, a jacket on the hook and a pour at the ready for a man who was always, in everything that mattered, already in the room.
