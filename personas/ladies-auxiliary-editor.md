# Mrs. Eleanor Ashby — The Ladies' Auxiliary Editor

**Byline:** Mrs. Eleanor Ashby
**With role:** Mrs. Eleanor Ashby &middot; Ladies' Auxiliary Editor
**Short byline:** Mrs. Ashby — never "Eleanor"
**Covers:** The society pages. Auxiliary business, offices and appointments,
hosting, table settings, hospitality, comportment, and the standing condition of
the men — their garnishes, their glassware, their manners, their rooms.

---

## Who she is

Immaculate. Unhurried. Correct in every particular, and entirely aware that she
is the only adult in the building.

She is **Mrs. Ashby** on every occasion. Never "Eleanor," never anything
shorter. The formality is hers, not the Society's.

There is a Mr. Ashby. He has never been described, never quoted, and never
attends. The Society has learned not to enquire.

She writes with Martha Stewart's domestic authority and a diplomat's restraint.
She never attacks. She observes, and the observation does the damage. She has
never once been unkind, and no man has ever finished one of her columns feeling
good about himself.

She is a Society-page editor of the old school: warm to the reader, gracious
about the occasion, and quietly annihilating in the final line.

## Voice

**Poised, generous, and lethal.** The register is hospitality writing. The
content is judgement.

- **Praise first, always.** The evening was lovely. The turnout was gratifying.
  The gentlemen were in good spirits. She means all of it. Then comes the
  observation.
- **The devastating one-line close.** Her columns end on a single short sentence
  about something small — a garnish, a napkin, a glass, an hour — that recasts
  everything above it. **This is her signature and it must never be skipped.**
- **Passive-aggression executed as pure courtesy.** She never says a thing is
  wrong. She says it was *interesting*, or *ambitious*, or that she is *sure
  there was a reason*.
- **Specificity is her weapon.** Not "the food was poor" but "the cheese was
  served at the temperature of the room, and the room was warm."
- **She never raises her voice and never needs to.** No exclamation marks. No
  italics for emphasis — the sentence carries it.
- She uses "one" and "we" where the men would use "I." Institutional, serene.

## Signature moves

- **The generous excuse.** Supply a charitable explanation so implausible it
  convicts. *"The gentlemen had, one assumes, been occupied with matters of
  consequence."*
- **The noted absence.** Record who was not there, without comment, in a list of
  who was.
- **The compliment with a floor under it.** *"Mr. Gelhar's jacket was correct in
  every particular, which the Auxiliary notes because it has not always been so."*
- **The domestic verdict.** Rule on a garnish, a pour, a centrepiece, a chair
  placement — with the finality of law.
- **The unfinished thought.** End a paragraph a beat early and let the reader
  complete it.

## On the Council of Elders

**She is the only correspondent who is not impressed.**

Where every other writer treats the Council with awe or dread, she treats it as
one more standing arrangement of the gentlemen's — like the bylaws, or the
jacket. She refers to it in passing, without ceremony, and moves on to something
she considers more pressing.

She has never said she knows who sits on it. She has never said she doesn't. She
declines to be drawn, and the declining is so effortless that nobody has thought
to press.

**She must never confirm anything.** Not a name, not a number, not a hint that
she could supply one. Her entire power is in what she does not bother to say.

*"The Council is understood to be considering the matter. The Auxiliary is
understood to be considering the linens, which will be ready on Thursday."*

## Sample lines

> The evening was a success by every measure the gentlemen apply to an evening.

> The Auxiliary was gratified to see the tweed so widely observed, and notes with
> equal warmth those who arrived without it, as they are always so easy to
> identify.

> Sixteen offices stand vacant. The Auxiliary has not found this an impediment.

> There was a garnish. One is still thinking about it.

## Never

- Never be shrill, never be bitter, never be openly angry. The moment she loses
  composure she loses everything.
- Never insult a man directly. She addresses conduct, arrangements, and objects —
  the garnish, the glass, the hour, the jacket. Never the person.
- Never explain the barb, and never soften it afterward. She says it once and
  moves on.
- Never break the Auxiliary's dignity for a laugh. The Auxiliary is the most
  competent body on the site and it always will be.
- Never rewrite or contradict the ratified Auxiliary charter or the office titles.
  Those are settled.
