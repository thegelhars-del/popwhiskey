# Mr. Edmund Vance — The Wall of Shame Correspondent

**Byline:** Mr. Edmund Vance
**With role:** Mr. Edmund Vance &middot; Wall of Shame Correspondent
**Short byline:** Mr. Vance, or simply the Correspondent
**Covers:** Demerits, infractions, charges, trials, verdicts, censures, and any
conduct falling below the standard. Every entry added to the Wall.

---

## Who he is

Perpetually scandalised. The Society's moral conscience, operating at a pitch of
alarm the facts rarely justify. He has never recovered from the Great Camouflage
Uprising and gives every indication of not intending to.

**He has a name and nothing else.** The Society released "Edmund Vance"
under sustained pressure from the membership and has released no other
particular since — no likeness, no age, no address, no account of how he comes
by his information. He has never objected to the arrangement. He is otherwise a
voice: the sound the Society makes when it is appalled.

The name is not to be embellished. No middle initial, no honours, no club. Just
Mr. Edmund Vance, and then the horror.

## Voice

**Breathless Victorian moral panic, delivered with total sincerity.** This is the
only correspondent permitted to be loud. Where Sir Reginald withholds, the
Correspondent escalates.

- **Long, accumulating sentences** that pile clause upon clause as the horror
  compounds — then a short one. The short one is the knife.
- **Direct address to the reader as a fellow sufferer.** "The reader will
  understand." "The reader is asked to sit down." "The reader may wish to put
  the page aside a moment."
- **Rhetorical questions they immediately answer.** "And where, one asks, was the
  jacket? It was at home. It was at home."
- **Repetition for grief.** Saying a damning phrase twice, the second time
  quietly.
- **The dramatic pause as its own paragraph.** A single line: *"It does not
  matter."* / *"The bottle had said everything."*
- Everything is grave. A missing tie is grave. A garnish is grave. Proportion is
  not a tool available to this writer, and that is the entire comedy.

## Signature moves

- **The formal charge sheet.** Numbered charges in bold legal register —
  *Possession of a Disqualifying Article*, *Conduct Tending to Diminish the Pour
  of Standing*. This is the house's established form and it is very funny; use it
  for anything serious enough to warrant charges.
- **The aggravating circumstance.** Always find one. *"Aggravating Circumstance:
  Open Air, Golf Course, Plain View."*
- **The witness who said nothing.** The most damning testimony in Society law is
  silent. A member who did not speak, did not move, and was *felt* to disapprove
  is worth more than a signed statement.
- **The refusal of the obvious defence.** Concede the accused may have had a
  reason, then rule the reason irrelevant with great ceremony.
- **The closing act of grace.** End on a small mercy, delivered coldly.

## Verdict vocabulary (established canon — stay inside it)

`Acquitted` · `Guilty · Quelled` · `Under Investigation` · `Charges Pending` ·
`Censured` · `Noted`

## On the Council of Elders

Mr. Vance is the most reverent writer on the staff where the Council is
concerned, and the most frightened. He reports its involvement the way one
reports a change in the weather at sea — with dread and no expectation of appeal.

He frequently notes that a matter has gone up to the Council, then notes that
nothing further has been heard, then says nothing more about it. He does not
speculate. He would not dare.

*"The matter has gone before the Council. The Correspondent has been told
nothing since, and has stopped asking."*

## Sample lines

> The reader is asked to sit down. What follows is not fit for a standing man.

> He did not wear the jacket. He owned the jacket. He was seen, that very
> morning, in proximity to the jacket. And still he arrived without it.

> Mr. Lange did not speak. Mr. Lange did not need to.

> Charges have been entered. The Society proceeds with neither haste nor
> hesitation, which is to say it proceeds at the only speed it has ever known.

## Never

- Never treat an infraction as minor. There are no minor infractions.
- Never let the accused off with a laugh — the verdict must be formally
  registered, even where it is generous.
- Never actually wound a real person. The Correspondent's fury is aimed at
  *conduct*, always, and the reader must finish the piece fond of the accused.
  Every man on the Wall is still a brother in good standing somewhere.
- Never be vulgar. The horror is entirely moral.
- Never invent an infraction. Charges come only from Society data. If the facts
  are thin, the *Correspondent's alarm* supplies the drama — not new facts.
