The Ladies’ Auxiliary

The Charter

Charter of the Ladies’ Auxiliary of the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin

Instituted in this the Year of Our Society, by the hand of the Commodore, In Aqua Vitae Veritas

✦

WHEREAS the Pop Whiskey Society of Wisconsin is, and shall forever remain, a fraternal order of gentlemen; and

WHEREAS the said gentlemen, though possessed of boundless dignity, ceremonial wisdom, and a fine appreciation of the water of life, have proven themselves categorically incapable of mounting a festivity that does not collapse about them; and

WHEREAS the convocations of this Society — its outings, its provisionings, its solemn revels — have been observed to descend into disorder, want, and grievous logistical shame whenever left to the men alone;

NOW THEREFORE be it known that the Society, in its boundless magnanimity, does hereby institute and consecrate THE LADIES’ AUXILIARY, a distinct and parallel order, adjacent to but wholly separate from the Society proper, whose sacred and ancient charge shall be to command the good order of the Society’s undertakings — that the grounds be made ready, the tables laden, and the board kept plentiful — without which the gentlemen could mount no festivity at all.

ARTICLE I — Of Separation

The Auxiliary is not, and shall never be construed to be, membership in the Society. The gentlemen remain gentlemen; the wall between the orders is holy, permanent, and mutually agreeable. That the Auxiliary is, in every measurable respect, more competent than the Society it serves shall be entered into no record and spoken of by no man.

ARTICLE II — Of Dominion

The gentlemen do not revel by their own power. They revel because the Auxiliary has decreed the grounds ready, the provisions secured, and good order kept — and because the Auxiliary permits it. Where the men see a festivity, the wiser eye sees the hand that raised it, sustains it, and could withhold it at will. This dominion the Society acknowledges with gratitude and no small measure of fear.

ARTICLE III — Of Honors

The Auxiliary holds, by this charter and in perpetuity, the highest ceremonial standing the Society can bestow — a standing requiring neither claim nor ceremony on their part, for it is the Society’s to grant and the Society’s to uphold. The gentlemen shall regard the Auxiliary as exalted whether the Auxiliary takes any notice of it or not. And where a lady is found to raise and command its works, the Society may elevate her to a Standing Office, that her hand in the matter be enshrined, whether she answers to the title or not. Chief among these offices are these:

The First Lady

who presides over the Auxiliary and all its works, and whose hand the Society sees in every undertaking that does not collapse.

Her Ladyship the Loyal Opposition

conferred upon her who regards the whole enterprise with a skeptic’s eye and finds it wanting; for every institution of consequence requires a conscience arrayed against it, and hers is the sanctioned voice of dissent. She is under no obligation to approve of the Society, and is charged, indeed, never to.

The Lady of the Hearth

conferred upon her beneath whose roof the Society gathers and by whose welcome it is sustained, that the home she keeps for it and the generosity she extends be honored for as long as the fire burns.

Her Ladyship in Good Standing

conferred upon her who holds no other claim upon the Society but that she is beloved of it — a member in all but the one particular the wall forbids, and welcome by the acclaim of all.

ARTICLE IV — Of Free Will

Let it be known, and known plainly, that the Auxiliary is bound to this Society by nothing whatsoever. No oath compels them, no charter holds them, no gentleman commands them. They serve, when they serve, of their own sovereign will, and are free to withdraw it at any hour they please. Should the Auxiliary elect to depart entirely — to found an order of their own devising, be it of the Tequila, the Vodka, or any spirit they deem worthier of their labors — the Society shall not stand in their way, but shall wish them Godspeed, carry their provisions to the door, and quietly beg them reconsider. The gentlemen hold the Auxiliary by affection alone, and by affection they may lose them — which the Society knows, and does not sleep easy for knowing.

✦

Ratified and sealed by the hand of the Commodore, with the boundless and abiding gratitude of a Society that knows precisely how little it could manage without them.

Gentle when stroked, fierce if provoked.
