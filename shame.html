Disciplinary Record

Wall of Shame

The Society is not without compassion. But the Society is not without memory. Herein are recorded those whose conduct — or conspicuous lack thereof — required formal proceedings.

Acquitted
Jeffrey Schuster
Chronic AWOL · Case No. 001
The first member in Society history to face formal charges — and the first to be saved by a vote of his peers. Accused of chronic absenteeism and serial ditching of club functions, Schuster stood trial before the full membership. The verdict: acquitted by the slimmest of margins, carried across the finish line by a combination of personal charm, creative excuses, and what one member described as "weapons-grade bullshit." An OG member who perpetually hangs on by the thinnest of threads, Schuster remains a man of contradictions — unreliable at the bar, yet undeniably formidable at the billiards table, where his prowess in 3-ball commands genuine, if grudging, respect.
Guilty · Quelled
Gary R. Gelhar
The Great Camouflage Uprising · Sedition Against the Dress Code
In a brazen act of sartorial insurrection, the accused formally proposed replacing the Society's sacred tweed sport coat with camouflage. For all members. As official policy. The Commodore set down his glass. The Council of Elders exchanged glances of barely contained alarm. The motion was debated and quelled with swift, decisive unanimity. The tweed held. The bylaws stood firm. The accused accepted the verdict with characteristic good humor and continues to wear camouflage in a personal capacity. The uprising has been absorbed into Society lore as both a cautionary tale and one of its greatest stories.
Under Investigation
Maisie Gelhar
Exploitation of the Curling Loophole · Infiltration by Competence · Pending Investigation
The charges against Maisie Gelhar are as follows: that she did, with deliberate and suspicious proficiency, insert herself into a Society curling event under the guise of instruction; that she did captain said team to victory using skills that nobody knew she had; and that she did exploit the resulting championship with calculated precision to gain entry into an organization that has admitted exactly two women in its entire history. The smile, the happy-go-lucky demeanor, the impeccable bookkeeping — the membership now views all of it through the lens of a woman who had a plan. The books are balanced, yes. But at what cost? It should be noted that Maisie is the founder's daughter, which raises serious questions about whether this entire operation was orchestrated from the very top. The founder has declined to comment. Charges are pending. The investigation continues. The jacket, regrettably, has already been issued and cannot be recalled. Well played, Maisie. The Society is watching.
Charges Pending
Mr. Connor Buechel
The Pink Whitney Incident · The Aberlour Affair · Charges Pending
The accused did, on a golf course and in plain view, transport upon his person and by his own arrangement one (1) bottle of Pink Whitney — a flavored vodka of suspect provenance — discovered in the course of a pour of Aberlour A'bunadh among gentlemen of standing. The matter is rendered the graver still by the silent, unspoken, and unanimously felt disappointment of Mr. Scott Lange, the Society's resident authority on vodka, who was present at the scene. Charges have been formally entered. The membership awaits the proceedings.

Mr. Connor Buechel

Charges Pending · The Pink Whitney Incident

Charges pending. The Society proceeds with neither haste nor hesitation.

Let the record show.

Upon a golf course — a setting which has long served as a cradle of decorum and a proving ground of taste — Mr. Connor was extended that most civilised of courtesies: a pour. A pour of Aberlour A'bunadh, cask strength, sherry-matured, a Speyside of evident pedigree, drawn from a bottle that has cost a man money and earned a man peace.

Witnessed by several members in good standing.

What followed is a matter the Society approaches with appropriate candour. The hour was advanced. The day had been generous. The membership cannot, in honest conscience, swear whether Mr. Connor refused the pour outright, accepted it and offered his own alternative alongside, or some combination thereof which the historical record can no longer reconstruct.

It does not matter.

The Society pauses here, in the prose, that the foregoing sentence might be permitted to settle upon the reader as it has settled upon the membership.

It does not matter, because the offense is not located in what was poured, refused, or exchanged. The offense is located in the simple, irrefutable, and witnessed fact that Mr. Connor's golf bag contained, on his person and by his choosing, a bottle of Pink Whitney.

The Society shall now address Pink Whitney, as the matter cannot be properly tried without it.

Pink Whitney is a vodka. The Society uses the word vodka generously, and under protest. It is flavored with pink lemonade. It is bottled in a hue not occurring in nature, not present in the visible spectrum as understood by serious men. It is packaged in a manner that suggests, strongly, that the manufacturer was uncertain whether it ought to be sold beside the spirits or beside the juice boxes — and split, in the end, the difference. It is the official beverage of bachelorette parties, of poolside afternoons in jurisdictions the Society does not recognise, and of birthday celebrations for children whose ages are denoted, on the cake, by a single candle. It tastes, by all credible accounts, the way a scented candle smells. It is not consumed. It is survived.

That such a bottle existed within the same golf bag as gentlemen had gathered to drink Aberlour is, the Society submits, the charge.

A Word on the Aggravating Presence of Mr. Scott Lange

The Society would be remiss were it not to enter into these proceedings the further and grievous fact that Mr. Scott Lange, member in good standing and the Society's resident authority on vodka, was present at the scene.

Mr. Lange did not, at the time of the offense, speak. He did not raise objection. He did not, by any account the Society has been able to gather, alter the position of his head, the set of his jaw, or the cadence of his breath.

And yet the membership felt it.

Each gentleman present has since confirmed, independently, that he registered — at the moment of the bottle's revelation — the unmistakable and silent shaking of Mr. Lange's head in the direction of disappointment. The shake was not visible. The shake was not audible. The shake was felt, in the manner that gentlemen of standing feel such things.

Mr. Lange said nothing because Mr. Lange did not need to. The bottle had said everything.

The Charges, Formally Entered

  • Possession of a Disqualifying Article, the said article being one (1) bottle of Pink Whitney, transported by Mr. Connor's own arrangement to a setting where gentlemen had gathered.
  • Conduct Tending to Diminish the Pour of Standing, whether by refusal, substitution, parallel offering, or some confluence thereof which the witnesses can no longer disentangle and which the Society no longer requires disentangled.
  • Provocation of Silent Disappointment in a Member of Specialised Authority, the said member being Mr. Scott Lange, the said disappointment entered into the record by the unanimous and independent recollection of the membership present.
  • Aggravating Circumstance: Open Air, Golf Course, Plain View.

Mr. Connor's name is hereby affixed to this Wall, where it shall remain pending such time as the Society, in its sole and unhurried judgement, deems the matter resolved. The Society does not anticipate resolution.

In closing: the Aberlour did not go to waste. The Society saw to that. As to the Pink Whitney — the bottle has not been seen since, and the Society considers this, on balance, the only act of grace performed that afternoon. Mr. Lange has declined to comment. Mr. Lange does not need to.
